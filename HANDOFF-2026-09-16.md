## What it is

287 US credit and debit cards scored by what they really pay at a **Costco warehouse** (MCC 5300) over a full month of $10,000 and $50,000 in spend. Caps, tiers and monthly reward ceilings are applied. It is an acquisition list: which cards to open.

## The one number that matters

```
Upside% = Costco% at $50k/month  +  debit-pay stack (1.80%)  −  annual fee
```

The page sorts by **Upside%**. `Net now` beside it counts only a **verified** debit rail. An amber number means the rail is unconfirmed but still in play.

## Standing rules (Medi's, these override any research)

1. **Never score by the headline rate.** Payment rail and Costco MCC decide value, not the advertised percent.
2. **Costco is MCC 5300 warehouse club, not 5411 grocery.** Most grocery bonuses exclude it. Venmo is the known exception.
3. **Never kill a lead.** If it is not explicitly written either way, leave it in play as `?`. N requires the issuer to say no.
4. **Non-US is a hurdle, not a disqualifier.** Show the real capped rate with a badge.
5. **Medi's word is the record.** Rulings go in `rebuild/medi-rulings.json` and are applied last. Current rulings: Citi Costco Anywhere **does** accept Taekus; Citi Double Cash does not.
6. **Raw data only.** No advice panels, no "biggest catch" callouts on the page. The interpretive accordion was removed on his instruction.
7. **Source quality.** A Y needs the issuer's own page, a dated first-hand report, or Medi. WalletHub answers and AI summaries are not evidence.

## Open questions, in priority order

1. **Does Taekus actually pay on a card-bill payment?** The whole top of the list rides on it. Forum evidence is negative: roughly 0% on an Amazon reload, about 2% on a hotel. A $5 live test on the Venmo card settles it. If it pays nothing, set `TAEKUS = 0` in `build.py` and the ranking collapses to the gross rate.
2. **Which Taekus product does Medi hold**, personal or business? Citi blocks commercial debit.
3. **Work the 112 unknown rails.** Use `rebuild/DEBITPAY-DISCOVERY-PROMPT.md`. It lists nine ways to verify without holding the card: the CFPB agreement database, the issuer's own pages, the payment IVR menu, the processor's biller directory, walk-in networks, app screenshots, the Wayback Machine, pre-sales chat and the fee schedule.
4. **Capital on Tap: does the 2% survive the debit rail?** The 2% needs weekly AutoPay from a bank account; debit payments are manual. Their terms suggest a manual payment does not cancel enrollment, but nobody has confirmed the combination.
5. **Credit limits.** A $50,000 month needs either a large line or several payoff cycles. Capital on Tap advertises up to $50,000 but typical approvals are far lower.
6. **Reddit was never swept.** It is network-blocked in this environment. r/churning and r/manufacturedspending remain unread.

## Known process failures, so they do not repeat

- **Ranked by the wrong number.** Capital on Tap sat at row 34 of 252 because the page sorted by gross Costco rate. Whenever a scoring rule changes, check what the *page* ranks by, not just the data.
- **One weak source became a headline.** Wells Fargo was listed at 3.80 net on a WalletHub user answer.
- **Inference treated as fact.** 189 cards carried a debit-pay N that came from one prose sentence. 97 were flipped back to leads.
- **Double-counted stack.** Research agents returned percentages that already included the 1.80; the build added it again. Agent output must be card-rate only.
- **Agents that delegate.** Two research agents spawned sub-agents and exited without writing their file. Tell them to do the work themselves.
